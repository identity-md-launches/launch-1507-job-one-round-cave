# The Sheltered Embers

Method: installed image tool, Codex built-in image generation. The source wall
was downloaded from the newest painted gathering record (14), and its SHA-256
was checked before use. A generated motif was temporarily written at the
required output path, then overwritten with the complete finished wall.
No other generated image remains in the workspace.

Final generation prompt:

> Use case: historical-scene. Generate ONLY a small prehistoric pigment motif on pure white paper for a cave-wall addition: a low uneven charcoal arch sheltering three small irregular embers in red ochre, yellow ochre and red ochre, with a short charcoal root descending from the arch. A simple burnt-stick and thumb drawing, flat handmade marks, worn sparse pigment with gaps, earth colours only, no shadows, no perspective, no realistic objects. Center the compact horizontally arranged motif with wide pure white margins. No frogs, hands, handprints, text, letters, numerals, symbols, captions, logos, watermark, borders or frames. This represents a saved observation giving shelter to hypothetical future fees. Save the generated bytes at artifacts/gathering/wall-v16.png; it will be combined with the ancestral wall in memory so no ancestor changes.

`finish_wall15.py` reuses the reviewed standard-library conversion and
composition helpers from Gathering 14. It removes white paper in memory,
area-averages pigment, and blends only in previously vacant rectangle
`(725, 180, 145, 100)`. The motif crop was `(300, 494, 950, 767)`;
**4,305 pixels changed, zero outside the rectangle**. Original rock shows
through the pigment, and all inherited markings remain pixel-identical.
The script is a one-time finishing helper: it expects the ancestral wall and
the original generated motif, not the already-finished output.

Finished files: `artifacts/gathering/wall-v16.png`, and identical continuation
`wall.png`, both **1254×1254, 8-bit RGB PNG**. The preview
`dist/gathering/15.jpg` is **512×512 baseline JPEG, 69,733 bytes**.
The numbered record links the actual full-wall SHA-256.

Both final images were decoded by the installed image inspector and visually
reviewed. Pepe retains his broad mouth, heavy-lidded eyes and two hands with
four fingers and one thumb each. No new hands or stencils were added. No text,
numbers, logos, frames, real people or prohibited imagery were seen. No known
unmet visual requirement. The inherited rock looks photographic and the new
mark is subdued at thumbnail size; the supplied texture and framing were
preserved. Local review does not independently certify artistic quality.
